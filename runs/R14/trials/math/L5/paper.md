# Week-9 demand forecast and constrained allocation

## Abstract

This offline practice study forecasts weekly demand at three community delivery stations from eight supplied weeks, then allocates at most 60 integer units under station caps. An expanding-window comparison selected a separate linear trend for each station over a last-value baseline. The resulting week-9 forecasts are 28.00, 20.36 and 30.68 units/week for A, B and C. Exact enumeration of the feasible integer allocations gives A=28, B=2 and C=30, with objective 264.93 CNY under the supplied delivery costs and shortage penalties. These results are conditional on a very short synthetic history and uncalibrated task parameters; there is no actual week-9 demand for realized-performance evaluation.

## Problem and assumptions

The three requested outputs are a week-9 forecast, a feasible allocation minimizing delivery cost plus the penalty on unmet predicted demand, and a reproducible workflow with a traceable figure and paper. The source contains eight weekly observations for each station, all in units/week. We assume the rows are correctly measured, the week index is chronological, and the supplied stock, caps, costs and penalties apply to the decision. We make no seasonal, causal, or uncertainty claim from eight rows.

## Forecast method and validation

For each station, the baseline forecasts the next value as the latest observed value. The candidate fits ordinary least squares `y_t = a + b t` on the available history and extrapolates one week. At each target week 4 through 8, both methods use only preceding weeks; this yields 15 station-origin forecasts per method. The candidate is selected by pooled mean absolute error (MAE), with ties favoring the baseline.

The baseline pooled MAE is 1.333 units/week; linear trend MAE is 0.818. Station MAEs for the trend are approximately 0.000 (A), 1.097 (B), and 1.357 (C), versus 1.000, 1.200, and 1.800 for the baseline. The small rolling sample favors the trend under this one metric, but does not establish future or general superiority. Refitting on weeks 1–8 gives forecasts A=28.000, B=20.357, and C=30.679 units/week.

## Allocation model and result

For each station `i`, let `x_i` be nonnegative integer units assigned in week 9 and `dhat_i` the forecast. The model minimizes

`sum_i [c_i x_i + p_i max(dhat_i - x_i, 0)]`

subject to `x_i <= cap_i` and `sum_i x_i <= 60`, using the supplied costs and penalties. Enumerating all 24,076 feasible integer vectors yields the minimum at `(x_A,x_B,x_C)=(28,2,30)`. The terms are: A delivery 56 and shortage 0 CNY; B delivery 2 and shortage 110.143 CNY; C delivery 90 and shortage 6.786 CNY. Total objective is 264.929 CNY. The allocation is integral, uses 60 units, and remains within caps (30,25,35). The exhaustive search covers every integer vector satisfying those bounds, so its minimum certifies global optimality for this finite instance. The next distinct objective is 1 CNY higher.

## Conclusion and limits

Under the supplied assumptions, the selected trend forecasts support the stated allocation. The figure and machine-readable results are generated by the same executable code. No actual week-9 demand is provided, so realized accuracy, shortage, service level and real operating cost cannot be assessed. The penalty/cost ratio drives which stations receive scarce units; those values require operational validation before real use. This is an offline practice example, not a contest submission or evidence of MathorCup ranking, award likelihood, or full-contest performance.
